##### [A TACKY, MYSTERY THEME, FULL OF SYNTH-Y THERAMIN]

##### [CELIA and GEORGIE are recording remotely, badly]

#### GEORGIE

Hello and welcome to another episode of Sheeple Chase, the podcast where we question everything, including our life choices. I'm Georgie Barker... 

#### CELIA

And I'm Celia Ripley. 

#### GEORGIE

So, how are you doing today, Celia? 

#### CELIA

I'm okay. Not much on outside of family stuff. 

#### GEORGIE

Yeah? You look like you need a rest. 

#### CELIA

Wow! I skip mascara one time and suddenly I'm the walking dead! 

#### GEORGIE

In a hot Twilight way, not a night-of-the-living-dead zombie way! I just meant maybe don't run any marathons today. 

#### CELIA

But what if I *have* to, Georgie? 

#### GEORGIE

Why would you *have* to run a marathon? 

#### CELIA

I don't know, what if some disaster knocked out all the world's infrastructure and I had to send a really urgent message to someone 26 miles away? 

#### GEORGIE

God, could you imagine? You wouldn't even get to stop for lunch! You have to *run* right through *lunch!*

##### [She shudders] 

#### CELIA

I feel like you might have other priorities than lunch at that point... 

#### GEORGIE

Hard disagree. 

#### CELIA

You know what? I retract my statement. You absolutely would still be obsessed with food. 

#### GEORGIE

Damn right. So on that note, while I fish out my most tastefully erotic expanding joggers, let's hear from today's sponsor!

##### [banking app ping]

#### CELIA

_(flat)_ Darn, I forgot to cancel a subscription.

##### [banking app ping]

#### CELIA

Oh, and that one --

##### [banking app ping]

#### CELIA

And that one --

##### [banking app ping]

#### CELIA

And that one! _(sighs)_ Don't you just hate wasting money on old subscriptions? 

#### GEORGIE

_(peppy)_ No! Because I have SubSub, the subscription service management service subscription! 

#### CELIA

The subscription subservice sub... what? 

#### GEORGIE

SubSub! The subscription service management service subscription! With SubSub, I can manage all my subscriptions in one place, so I never lose track of what I'm paying for. 

#### CELIA

That could save me so much money. 

#### GEORGIE

At only 49.99 a month it pays for itself! Literally, because your SubSub sub is also managed through your SubSub account. 

#### CELIA

Wow, sounds like I really need a subscription service management subsc--

#### GEORGIE

Subscription service management service subscription. 

#### CELIA

Subscription management service --

#### GEORGIE

Just say SubSub. 

#### CELIA

SubSub.

_(increasingly deadpan)_ SubSub.

_(very deadpan)_ SubSub.

##### [jingle]

#### GEORGIE

So, Celia, now I'm all dressed to flee from any government agents, which conspiracy theory have you brought for us today? 

#### CELIA

I think you're going to like this one...

_(narrating)_ The year is 1964. Rock and roll rules the airwaves, lead paint is everywhere and the cold war is getting hot... what a time for the UK to elect a KGB spy as prime minister!

##### [Excessively spooky sting]

#### GEORGIE

Harold Wilson? 

#### CELIA

Yeah, today we're looking at UK Prime Minister Harold Wilson and whether he was a Soviet puppet, planted in Downing Street through a combination of manipulation, assassination, and good, old-fashioned corruption. Spoilers: probably not. 

#### GEORGIE

So what, the Tories had just hired John Le Carré as their new spin doctor or something? 

#### CELIA

Interestingly, at first glance it does seem as if the story comes from a legitimate source. In '63, a KGB agent named Anatoliy Golitsyn defected to the CIA, and gave them a list naming Soviet agents in the US and UK. 

#### GEORGIE

And Harold Wilson was one of them? 

#### CELIA

Apparently. Now, obviously, when a KGB agent wanders into the CIA offices and starts naming British politicians like he's hosting Newsnight, MI5 don't immediately assume they're *all* spies. But they *do* start asking questions, like why he wore so many Lithuanian-designed raincoats. 

#### GEORGIE

Sorry, that like an innuendo thing? "Does he... you know... wear Lithuanian raincoats?" 

#### CELIA

The raincoats weren't even Lithuanian. They were made in Leeds. 

#### GEORGIE

Notorious hotbed of international communist conspiracy. 

#### CELIA

You say that, but the guy who owned the factory was a Lithuanian immigrant and someone had already told someone who told MI5 that *that* guy had played chess with a guy who *might* have talked to a different guy who was in the KGB. 

#### GEORGIE

Ha! What was the real reason? 

#### CELIA

No, that's the actual real reason. At least, according to Peter Wright, the MI5 agent assigned to the case. 

#### GEORGIE

Wow! Okay, so if they were already investigating him for the raincoat-chess connection, they must have been totally psyched when someone actually named Wilson as a double agent? 

#### CELIA

You would have thought so, but it sounds like they didn't really consider it that big a deal. In his memoirs, Peter Wright said they thought it was rubbish, but because it came from the head of the CIA's counterintelligence division, they "had no choice" but to file it somewhere. 

#### GEORGIE

Huh. Apparently I would be a terrible spy, because I would *definitely* have taken that more seriously than the whole communist raincoat thing. 

#### CELIA

Well, don't write off your future career just yet. There were a couple of mitigating circumstances. One was that the CIA Counterintelligence chief, Angleton, was a known bullshitter with one agent describing him as "not above exaggeration" --

#### GEORGIE

Brutal putdown, for a spy. 

#### CELIA

-- and Wright said he was known to "manufacture evidence where none existed." 

#### GEORGIE

Devastating. 

#### CELIA

And Angleton said he *"definitely"* had evidence, but would only share it if MI5 pinky-promised not to tell its government it was being run by a Soviet agent. 

#### GEORGIE

And I'm guessing MI5 didn't go for it? 

#### CELIA

Ha! No. 

#### GEORGIE

Even with Raincoat-gate? 

#### CELIA

I mean, they thought he was dodgy, but not like sliding-into-Khrushchev's-DMs dodgy. 

#### GEORGIE

So do *we* know now what evidence Angleton had? 

#### CELIA

He had the Russian defector, Golitsyn. Which sounds bad at first, but let's just say the official historian for MI5 described him as "an unreliable conspiracy theorist." 

#### GEORGIE

I'm sorry, what kind of job is "official MI5 historian"? All your sources are classified, so do all your footnotes just say "trust me"? 

#### CELIA

Pretty much. Golitsyn was claiming some wild stuff, like that Hugh Gaitskell was assassinated by the KGB to make way for Wilson. 

#### GEORGIE

Oh wow, was he? 

#### CELIA

Not unless the KGB invented lupus. 

#### GEORGIE

But wasn't this Golitsyn guy the one who identified the Cambridge Five spy ring? 

#### CELIA

Look, I'm not saying *none* of his info panned out, just that MI5 didn't trust him as far as they could throw him. 

#### GEORGIE

Hmmm. Sounds like they might have benefited from today's sponsor... 

#### CELIA

Worst segue yet...

##### [Ad jingle]

#### GEORGIE

With so many scammers online, it's easier than ever to lose your hardearned money to rogue e-course providers. Thankfully, you can learn how to avoid low quality, overpriced and irrelevant e-courses in a few short web seminars with Itsac Online.

Their patented ten-step method for avoiding online scams is so easy, *anyone* can follow it, and you can learn at your own pace with a monthly subscription or save big with a year's access for only £299.99.

Learn your lesson and save your money for the important things in life, like Itsac Online!

##### [ad outro]

#### GEORGIE

So where were we? 

#### CELIA

"Unreliable conspiracy theorist." 

#### GEORGIE

My kinda guy. So, given who else was named by this informant, *could* Harold Wilson have been a Soviet asset? Did MI5 miss something? 

#### CELIA

Doubtful. They were already looking into him, remember? 

#### GEORGIE

Because of the raincoats? 

#### CELIA

Because of the raincoats. And jokes aside, they already had him under constant surveillance because of that, bugs in his offices, in his house, his car... 

#### GEORGIE

So if he *had* been directly in touch with Russia, they'd definitely have known. 

#### CELIA

Oh yeah. 

#### GEORGIE

But what about *indirect* contact? Could he have used some sort of code word when he bought raincoats or something? 

#### CELIA

I mean, it's possible, but you kinda hope MI5 would notice. Codes are kind of their thing. 

#### GEORGIE

I thought their thing was suspecting entrepreneurial Lithuanians. 

#### CELIA

They can have two things. 

#### GEORGIE

So if they didn't think he was a Soviet spy, what were they bugging him for? 

#### CELIA

Apparently they were "concerned he might not be aware of the risk of being compromised." 

#### GEORGIE

So they decided to secretly compromise his privacy? 

#### CELIA

Pretty much. 

#### GEORGIE

_(singsong)_ Irony... 

#### CELIA

Yeah, so it turns out that as a person, Harold Wilson might have been the most boring Prime Minister ever. Plus, it backfired, because Wilson figured out he was being bugged. 

#### GEORGIE

Oh, so he *did* have spy instincts after all? 

#### CELIA

I mean, everybody close to him thought it was just paranoia. 

#### GEORGIE

But it's not paranoia if they really *are* out to get you! 

#### CELIA

Well, quite. 

#### GEORGIE

So, do you think this is what was really going on with the ghost thing? 

#### CELIA

The what? What's the ghost thing? 

#### GEORGIE

You don't know? 

#### CELIA

Know what? 

#### GEORGIE

Harold Wilson was absolutely *convinced* that Downing Street was haunted.

##### [Excessively spooky sting] 

#### CELIA

Really? 

#### GEORGIE

I mean, bear in mind, this is 50-year-old gossip from the Oxford University Labour Club. But... they say that he'd hear people whispering about him behind his back, but when he turned around, there'd be no one there. And there was this constant mechanical whirring, just on the edge of his hearing... It sounds like all that might actually have been real! No wonder he resigned so suddenly... 

#### CELIA

He actually resigned because he was ill, though, right? 

#### GEORGIE

Sure, but I doubt being haunted by MI5 helped. 

#### CELIA

I suppose not. 

#### GEORGIE

So, do you want to talk about the plotting as well? 

#### CELIA

The... plotting? 

#### GEORGIE

You know, the scheme to oust him as PM? I know there was a proper coup planned at some point, which I guess makes a bit more sense if people thought he was a paranoid, ghost-obsessed Soviet spy. 

#### CELIA

Well, that's the weird thing about this conspiracy theory. The rumours themselves resulted in three actual, real-life conspiracies against him. 

#### GEORGIE

Three? How does something like that happen *three* times!? 

#### CELIA

I mean, they might not have all been real, but the first one probably was. That was in 1968 and was orchestrated by the head of the International Publishing Corporation, Cecil King, who was also the chairman of the *Daily Mirror.* 

#### GEORGIE

Classic *Daily Mirror.* 

#### CELIA

It didn't go brilliantly. Apparently one of King's journalists, Cudlipp, happened to have met the head of the Armed Forces, Lord Mountbatten, so they arranged a visit between Cudlipp, King, Lord Mountbatten and one of Mounbatten's mates who was some random called Sir Solly. 

#### GEORGIE

That name doesn't exactly scream political mastermind does it? 

#### CELIA

So, King turns up and says, "Lads, the country's on the brink of ruin, the government's about to collapse, there's gonna be blood in the streets and the army will need to take over. You up to it?" Mountebatten thinks about it, but then this Sir Solly guy says Mountbatten is no traitor, so that's the end of it and King is kicked out. 

#### GEORGIE

So that was the whole plot? A meeting that could have been an email? 

#### CELIA

I mean, King did publish a front-page call for Wilson to be removed from office "by any means necessary," but he was fired from *The Mirror* for it. Then Wilson was re-elected in 1974 and allegedly history repeated itself. 

#### GEORGIE

How far did they get this time? 

#### CELIA

Well, we don't really know if they were plotting at all. Mostly it was just Mountebatton holding training exercises in really public places without any warning, like a show of strength, maybe. 

#### GEORGIE

I mean, that is pretty rude, and I could see how Wilson would get a bee in his already bugged bonnet over it. 

#### CELIA

Especially since a BBC documentary revealed multiple ex-military personnel *were* building up private armies to march against him if he was outed as a Soviet plant. 

#### GEORGIE

Ah. Again, I think I might be team Wilson on this one. 

#### CELIA

It gets worse. At the time a pair of journalists were secretly recording him with a bugged briefcase --

#### GEORGIE

Christ, who *wasn't* secretly recording him? 

#### CELIA

-- and MI5 were feeding information on him to the Tories, so they could undermine him before the election. And his private secretary was doing the same thing for Thatcher directly. 

#### GEORGIE

Good grief... 

#### CELIA

Oh, and you know the raincoat guy? Apparently he bought the secretary a house, so... 

#### GEORGIE

Right, new conspiracy theory. Harold Wilson wasn't a Soviet spy, but he was being cursed by a vengeful Tory wizard, disguising himself as a Lithuanian chess-playing raincoat seller, who magically convinced everyone that the biggest threat to national security was a boring lefty whose main passions were council housing and widow's pensions. 

#### CELIA

Makes about as much sense, doesn't it? So, what's the verdict? Was Harold Wilson a Soviet spy? 

#### GEORGIE

Not a chance. The KGB would have to queue for days behind all the other plots just get near him! 

#### CELIA

Even though he did try to open up trade with the Soviet Union at one point? 

#### GEORGIE

I recon that might be why the KGB assassinated Hugh Gaitskell. They reckoned Wilson will be a bit better for them, so they just nudged a few germs towards Gaitskell when he's over in Russia and --

#### CELIA

You do know you can't *catch* lupus, right?

##### [Beat.]

#### GEORGIE

I think it's quite clear that I did not know that, no. 

#### CELIA

Cool. And what about all the other plots against him? 

#### GEORGIE

Let's just say if even a tenth of them were real, the poor guy must have been wading through hidden cameras just to get to the loo at night. What about you? 

#### CELIA

I mean, there definitely were some plots against him, even if he did take a couple of things personally. And he can be forgiven for being twitchy given all the journalists, politicians and civil servants that have admitted to sabotaging his career. 

#### GEORGIE

But was he a spy? Our very own *Mancunian* candidate?

##### [Buh Dum Tss] 

#### CELIA

No, I think 1960s conservatives just didn't know the difference between wanting a slightly higher tax rate and full-on militaristic communism. 

#### GEORGIE

Plus ça change. 

#### CELIA

That's it for this week, folks. Uh, thank you for listening, please don't forget to rate, review us, compliment us however you like, Georgie and I will be back with a new mystery every week! 

#### GEORGIE

We might actually have a special guest for an episode soon. 

#### CELIA

Might we? Who? 

#### GEORGIE

I don't want to say too much right now, because it's not confirmed yet, but I'm pretty excited. 

#### CELIA

Well, there's a new mystery for you already. We didn't even have to wait for next week after all.
